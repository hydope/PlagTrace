How accurate is it?

About 1 in 5 flags was innocent in our first test, and paraphrase is not detected at all. Those are the two sentences that matter most on this page. Everything below is the evidence for them — including the intervals that show how uncertain the numbers still are.

Version: v0.5.0 (git tag v0.5.0, applied to the commit that published this page). Run date: 2026-10-24. Reproduce with node tests/metrics.mjs and node tests/heldout.mjs in the repo.

The flagging fix — and what it cost

Before the fix, the first 20-pair run had precision 0.82 and a false-positive rate of 0.18: a common-knowledge sentence and a template sentence were flagged. The fix, in src/sim.js: a single short passage alone is no longer enough to flag. A pair is flagged only if there are at least 2 distinct matches, or one match of at least 150 characters, or at least 90 matched characters covering at least 50% of Document A. In the UI, unflagged passages are still shown, labelled "Shown for context — not flagged".

The cost is visible above: recall fell from 1.00 to 0.57, because short shared sentences that used to count as flags no longer do. That trade — fewer accusations, more missed short copies — is deliberate for a student-facing tool, and it is stated here rather than hidden by keeping the old rule.

Held-out set — 30 pairs, written after the fix, not used to tune it

The main set above is no longer held-out: the flagging rule was changed after its first run. So a second set was written afterwards, on different topics (cooking, sport, astronomy, medicine, law, music, gardening, aviation, geology, film), and the rule was not changed again in response to its results.

Base rate — why no verdict, in numbers

In the test sets, 45–56% of pairs are true copies. In a real class, maybe 2% are. Using the held-out numbers (recall 0.67, false-positive rate 0.13) at 2% prevalence: out of 1,000 students, about 20 true copies and 980 innocent papers. The tool would flag about 13 of the true copies and about 131 innocent papers — about 91% of flags would be innocent. That is the strongest argument for the no-verdict stance on this site: at real base rates, a flag is mostly a prompt to look, never a judgement.

Wikipedia check — a smoke test, not a measurement

A verbatim paste of the first 600 characters of the Wikipedia Photosynthesis article returned 98.7% coverage, 1 match, 592 characters, band high. That is the easiest possible case — a verbatim paste of a fetched article — so it is called a smoke test here, not "measured live" accuracy. A paraphrased version of the same idea returned 0 matches.

What is NOT claimed

  • No PAN benchmark. PAN was not downloaded or run. No PAN numbers are claimed.
  • No web-wide accuracy. These sets compare supplied documents only. They say nothing about Chegg, CourseHero, or publisher databases.
  • No AI-detection accuracy. PlagTrace contains no AI-text detection. See Why no AI %.
  • Tuning disclosure: the flagging rule was changed after the first set's results (see above). The held-out set is the check against that tuning, and its worse false-positive rate (0.13, not 0.00) is published, not replaced.
