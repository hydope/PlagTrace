How this works, in one line — and what it cannot do

PlagTrace compares your text against documents you supply, or against Wikipedia and scholarly sources you opt to search. It never issues a verdict, and "no match" means "no match in the sources checked" — never "no match anywhere". See measured accuracy · What it cannot detect · Internet check limits

This is a screening aid. Only a human reviewer may decide misconduct, after giving the student a chance to respond.

    2 · No second document? Optional internet sources

    Privacy trade-off, read before clicking: the local check sends nothing anywhere. Clicking below is the only step that sends anything out — the search phrases taken from Document A go to Wikipedia, DOAJ, OpenAlex and, best effort, Semantic Scholar. Full documents are never uploaded; only short search phrases are sent, and the comparison itself still runs in your browser.

    Optional — find open copies of your cited articles (Unpaywall, DOI lookup only, not a similarity check):

    — fetches each cited article's abstract via OpenAlex/DOAJ and compares it locally. Abstracts only: full text is usually behind a publisher paywall or browser block, and any source that cannot be fetched is listed as not fetched, never as 0%.

    Fetched articles are added as reference sources above, with a link to each source. Untick or remove them like any other source. If any of those sources rate-limits you, that is reported — it is never read as "no match".

    Academic papers, read this first: this search covers Wikipedia, DOAJ and OpenAlex abstracts (plus Semantic Scholar when it responds) only. It does not check the journal articles, books, or student papers a university checker uses. A 0% here is not proof your paper is clean — do not submit based on it alone.
